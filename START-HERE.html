QUIETDNS / FOR YOUR MAC

Fewer ads.
One simple switch.

A small menu-bar app that changes your Mac's DNS to filter many ad and tracker domains. Turn it off to restore your saved settings.

Get started

  1. Double-click Install and Open.command in the project folder. It checks the packaged app, installs it in your personal Applications folder, and opens it. Look for the shield at the top of your screen.
  2. Click the shield, then Turn On Ad Blocking. AdGuard DNS is selected by default. macOS will ask for administrator approval.
  3. Restart your browser, game, or BlueStacks so it stops using old cached connections.
  4. To undo the change, click Turn Off & Restore DNS and approve the macOS prompt.

DNS settings stay in effect even when the app is closed or the Mac restarts. The app saves your previous settings before enabling.

What about BlueStacks?

It may block separate ad domains inside games if BlueStacks uses your Mac's DNS. That depends on the emulator and the game.

Enable QuietDNS, fully quit and reopen BlueStacks, then open AdGuard's test page in the browser inside BlueStacks. Check the AdGuard DNS result, then test your game separately.

Games can use their own DNS. First-party ads can remain, and rewarded videos may become unavailable. BlueStacks' own promotions are a separate feature. See the full guide for Android Private DNS options and their separate controls.

Know the limits

DNS filtering cannot reliably remove YouTube or other ads served from the same domain as the content. A browser content blocker, such as uBlock Origin in Firefox, can help with more browser ads.

VPNs, Private Relay, browser Secure DNS, and some apps can use a different resolver. On means filtering DNS is configured; it does not promise all traffic is filtered.

This version uses plain DNS. It adds no DNS encryption. Your selected provider receives domain lookups; review AdGuard's privacy policy. You can select your own trusted filtering server while the app is off.

Restore or uninstall

Turn the app off before deleting it. After it reports off, quit and move the app to Trash. There is no background service to remove.

If the app will not open, run Emergency Restore.command from the project folder. It restores the saved DNS settings and asks for administrator approval.

If another app changed DNS, ordinary restore will stop to protect those edits. Review your DNS, then use Restore saved DNS anyway… only if you want the original settings back. Keep the recovery file until restoration succeeds.

A locally built app is ad-hoc signed. A transferred copy may need to be rebuilt on its destination Mac. Do not disable macOS security checks.
